💰 DEBASEMENT — PRINT, BUY THE DEBT, INFLATE THE STOCK AWAY
From 1942 until the Fed–Treasury Accord in March 1951, the Fed pegged bills at 0.375% and the long bond at 2.5% and bought whatever the Treasury issued. CPI was 8% in 1946, 14% in 1947, 8% in 1948. Gross debt fell from about 119% of GDP to about 70% by the early 1950s. Primary surpluses and roughly 4% real growth did a large share of that work. Gold did not. It was fixed at $35 until 1971.

This button is that mechanism on today’s debt, with the peg off. CPI is the quantity equation on the new sliders: M2 growth + velocity change − real GDP growth. The 10-year is capped below that inflation. Buybacks have to be large enough to defend the cap — about $25B/month per 1 percentage point of CPI above the cap, per $8T that rolls each year, and QE willingness of 70 or higher. That rule is a model threshold, not a law. Under it, the preset ($110B/month, cap 3.25%, CPI 6.4%) holds. Cut the buying and the cap breaks, which is 2022, not 1946.

The ranking is purchasing power over the horizon, not the first-year tape. Long bonds can rally in year one as the cap is installed, then lose ground every year the coupon sits under inflation. Gold’s gain is mostly a one-time revaluation, then inflation plus about 1.5 points — not 40% a year for a decade. Elasticities, versus the live baseline: gold ≈ 7.5% per 1pp drop in the real policy rate, plus ≈ 1.05% per 1 DXY point, plus 0.7% per 1pp of extra M2. Silver 1.25× that first year. Miners 1.6×. Damped so the dollar and the real rate do not fully double-count.

💰🔥 INFLATE-THE-DEBT CALCULATOR CPI − Fed ≥ 1.5% / QE 70+ / DXY <98
The mechanism (fiscal dominance): When debt gets this large, a government has three ways out — grow out of it (rarely enough), default (never chosen by a reserve issuer), or inflate it away via financial repression: pin policy rates below inflation, cap the long end with QE, and let a negative real rate erode the debt's real value. The US did exactly this from 1946–1951 and again through the 1970s. This calculator runs that math on today's numbers — adjust any input.
